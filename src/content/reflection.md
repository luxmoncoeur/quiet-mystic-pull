> "I feel like kaya ko gandahan, but hindi ko kayang simulan."

I said that a lot of times, especially to myself. I always stared at a blank canvas for way too long, questioning myself if I'm really an "artsy" person.

Back then, I believed I had creativity in me. I knew I could do artworks, redesign this and that, do creative publication materials, and make everything look good. Give me something and I'd make it better. There's always been a perfectionist side in me that wanted one more edge, one more fix, one more "wait, parang may something pang kulang" before I could finally call it done.

But give me nothing to start with, and I freeze.

That was the part nobody saw. I always needed a reference point. A mood board, something to get inspiration, especially with the digital posters I usually make, where so much of the process takes time before a full theme comes to life. I was good at following a direction, but I wasn't sure I could create one.

Somewhere along the way, the imposter syndrome in me moved in. It made me question whether I was a creative person, or just someone who was good at getting inspired to create art.

I thought creativity meant having a clear vision from the start. I thought "real" artists didn't need references. And because I didn't fit that picture, I convinced myself that maybe my creativity was only half of the real thing.

---

Then I went to class from time to time. Listened through, followed along the way, and let my artworks show side by side.

Upon observing, one thing kept showing up. The sea.

Tsunami. Waves. Water. Almost everything I made drifted back to NIKI's "Tsunami," a song that has stayed close to my heart for a long time now. It's funny how a song named after something so destructive is the one that calms me down the most. I can feel that whenever my mind gets too loud, that song is the one I go back to. It speaks to me in a way that I still can't fully explain, like it understands something about me that I haven't put into words yet.

I never planned it as my theme. I didn't sit down and decide, "okay, magpaka ocean tayo." It just kept happening. One artwork had waves, then the next one did too, and I just realized that the sea had become the center of everything.

It's like NIKI's "Magnets": no matter how hard you push something away, it keeps pulling you back in, again and again. That's exactly how the waves felt throughout this course. Every time I tried to think of something new, my hands drifted back to the water.

Even during scribble art, the piece I struggled with the most, when my mind went completely blank and I had zero ideas what to make, I snuck it in by incorporating waves. When nothing else made sense in my artwork, the sea did.

At first, I wondered if that was a weakness, but the more I looked at it, the more I realized it wasn't repetition. It was honesty. I kept incorporating the sea because it was the one thing I didn't have to force.

Maybe that's what a pattern really is. Not a decision, but the pull of a mystic force.

<p class="pull">When I had nothing to think of, the sea became the one I carried.</p>

If there was a plot twist, it was really the scribble art.

Looking at my own doodles and being told to find an image inside them? *Nawindang talaga ako.* Like there was nothing to match, just random lines I made without meaning anything. I had to stop looking for the right image and just start trusting my instincts. For the first time, I realized I could create meaning out of something that didn't have any yet.

Coffee painting was another challenging artwork to do. The last time I painted with something unusual was in elementary, with okra and watercolor. This time, I only had one color. Everything depended on how concentrated or watered down the coffee was, so I had to pull it off meticulously.

Then came the last painting activity. I chose to reinterpret the Mona Lisa in Rococo style. I focused on what makes something Rococo: soft pastels, playful details, and elegance. I mixed my colors on a scrap of bond paper using color theory, and by the end, that palette almost looked like an artwork on its own. It made me genuinely happy, because it was proof of all the work nobody would see in the final piece, painted together with a lot of colors.

> Sometimes the beauty isn't in the finished artwork. It's in the mess you made getting there.

---

Looking back, I realized how little I actually knew.

Art isn't just making something pretty. It takes time, imagination, and thinking. Now I believe something can be art if we pour our hearts into it, if it pleases us, or if we put genuine effort into it.

Exploring beyond what I know taught me lessons that shape how I think, even outside art.

A stranger might see all the waves and think I just love the ocean, or that I'm just a big NIKI fan. They wouldn't be wrong. But they might miss that the sea calms my whole nervous system when things get rough like waves.

*Art made me braver: to explore and to take opportunities given to me. It also taught me to never stop imagining, because sometimes we imagine things long before we know how to make them real.*

Just like a tsunami: far out at sea, barely a swell. It only rises as it gets closer to the shore.

> Maybe that's who I am right now, still far from shore, still building up.

And like magnets, art will keep pulling me back. Again and again.
